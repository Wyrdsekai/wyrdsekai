Liquid Time-constant Networks

Ramin Hasani, Mathias Lechner, Alexander Amini, Daniela Rus, Radu Grosu

Abstract

We introduce a new class of time-continuous recurrent neural network models. Instead of declaring a learning system's dynamics by implicit nonlinearities, we construct networks of linear first-order dynamical systems modulated via nonlinear interlinked gates. The resulting models represent dynamical systems with varying (i.e., liquid) time-constants coupled to their hidden state, with outputs being computed by numerical differential equation solvers.

These neural networks exhibit stable and bounded behaviour, yield superior expressivity within the family of neural ordinary differential equations (ODE), and give rise to improved performance on time-series prediction tasks. To demonstrate these properties and the superior performance of our models, we conduct a series of experiments characterising the universality of the proposed networks, and benchmark them on time-series prediction tasks against state-of-the-art recurrent neural networks.

The continuous-time formulation produces models that are both more expressive and more parameter-efficient than fixed-time-constant counterparts, and the LTC's input-dependent time constants are the key element that makes them liquid: each neuron's reaction speed adapts to the input it is currently receiving.

Contributions

  • Define LTC neurons via input-dependent ODEs and prove bounded dynamics.
  • Show universal approximation on time-series under mild Lipschitz assumptions.
  • Benchmark on Walker, Hand-Gesture, Sequential MNIST, and Person Activity datasets — LTC matches or exceeds GRU/LSTM baselines with 5-10x fewer parameters.
  • Discuss numerical solvers (Fused, Hybrid, Adjoint) and the wallclock cost of continuous-time inference.
