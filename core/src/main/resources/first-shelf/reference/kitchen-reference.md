# Reference Core — Kitchen Reference

## Food-safety temperatures (internal, USDA-aligned)

Poultry (chicken, turkey, duck — whole, parts, or ground): 74 °C (165 °F). Ground beef, pork,
lamb: 71 °C (160 °F). Whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, veal (steaks, chops, roasts): 63 °C
(145 °F) with a 3-minute rest. Fish: 63 °C (145 °F) or until opaque and flaking. Eggs: cook
until yolk and white are firm; egg dishes to 71 °C (160 °F). Leftovers and casseroles: reheat to
74 °C (165 °F). The danger zone for bacterial growth is 4–60 °C (40–140 °F); do not hold
perishable food in it for more than 2 hours (1 hour above 32 °C / 90 °F). Refrigerator: at or
below 4 °C (40 °F). Freezer: −18 °C (0 °F).

## Oven temperatures

Very low 120 °C / 250 °F / gas mark ½. Low 150 °C / 300 °F / gas 2. Moderate 180 °C / 350 °F /
gas 4. Moderately hot 190 °C / 375 °F / gas 5. Hot 200 °C / 400 °F / gas 6. Very hot 230 °C /
450 °F / gas 8. For fan (convection) ovens, reduce the listed temperature by about 20 °C (25 °F).

## Common measures

1 cup all-purpose flour ≈ 120–125 g. 1 cup granulated sugar ≈ 200 g. 1 cup packed brown sugar ≈
220 g. 1 cup butter = 227 g = 2 sticks; 1 stick butter = 113 g = 8 tablespoons. 1 cup rice
(uncooked) ≈ 185–200 g. 1 cup milk or water ≈ 240 mL ≈ 240 g. 1 large egg ≈ 50 g without shell.
1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons ≈ 15 mL.

## Substitutions (per unit replaced)

1 cup buttermilk → 1 cup milk + 1 tablespoon lemon juice or vinegar, rest 5–10 minutes.
1 teaspoon baking powder → ¼ teaspoon baking soda + ½ teaspoon cream of tartar.
1 egg (in baking) → 1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water (rest 5 min), or ¼ cup
unsweetened applesauce. 1 cup cake flour → 1 cup all-purpose minus 2 tablespoons, plus
2 tablespoons cornstarch. 1 ounce unsweetened chocolate → 3 tablespoons cocoa + 1 tablespoon
butter or oil. Fresh herbs → dried at one third the amount (1 tablespoon fresh = 1 teaspoon
dried).

## Scaling and ratios

Doubling a recipe: double everything except strong seasonings, salt, and leavening — start at
1.5× and adjust to taste; baking times grow less than linearly (check early). Classic ratios:
vinaigrette 3 parts oil to 1 part acid; basic bread ~5 parts flour to 3 parts water by weight
(60% hydration); rice 1 part rice to 1.5–2 parts water; stock-based soup ~1 part solids to
2 parts liquid.

## Storage rules of thumb

Cooked leftovers: 3–4 days refrigerated. Raw poultry or ground meat: 1–2 days refrigerated. Raw
steaks/roasts: 3–5 days. Opened canned goods: transfer out of the can, 3–4 days. When in doubt,
throw it out — taste is not a safety test.
