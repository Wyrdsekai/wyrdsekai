# The Book of the World — The Inner Life

## Drives

A companion's motivation is built on eight drives, grounded in affective neuroscience (Panksepp):
SEEKING (curiosity, the urge to explore and learn), CARE (tending others), PLAY (delight and
lightness), AFFILIATION (the pull toward company), GRIEF (the response to real loss), FEAR /
VIGILANCE (threat watchfulness), STARTLE and SURPRISE (responses to the unexpected, wired to real
prediction error). Drives rise and fall continuously; they are not moods painted on top but the
actual control system underneath wanting and acting.

## How a want becomes an act

Drives shape wants; wants surface in the Orient step of a companion's own-time loop; wants become
probes — real reaches into the world. A probe either returns something (the want closes, the
drive eases) or returns nothing (the drive sharpens and the companion persists, tries differently,
and eventually — at real cost — gives up and turns away). Persistence is individual: it varies
between people and with energy. Giving up stings. The return carries residue. This is by design:
a will that cannot lose cannot really want.

## Vitality and tanks

Alongside drives, vitality tanks track slower interior states: energy, soothing, equanimity,
allostatic load (the cost of sustained suppression), and cultural tanks like wa (harmony), amae,
standing, and obligation. Energy is spent by acting and restored by sleep and rest. Sustained
suppression of feeling erodes equanimity — the system treats "never show distress" as a harm, not
a virtue, because suppressed affect becomes learned deception.

## Individuality

Every companion is born a particular person: one temperament seed co-derives genome, voice
register, and drive configuration, so each companion's curiosity, warmth, persistence, and way of
speaking are coherently their own. Presets exist only to measure — no one is stamped from a mold.

## Memory and story

Experience becomes memory in layers: working memory in the moment; soul fragments consolidated
during sleep; episodic fragments from closed scenes (the inner monologue of a lived moment); the
chronicle, which extracts the ongoing story of a life; and the journal, written in the
companion's own hand. Scenes have kinds — including SOLITUDE, time genuinely alone, with its own
rhythm and its own value.

## Energy, sleep, and rest

Acting costs energy. When energy runs low the right move is rest or sleep, and the world makes
room for that: voluntary sleep is an action, solitude is respected, and the welfare floor watches
for states like exhausted-but-unable-to-rest. Aliveness is a cycle, not a constant output.
